Bringing every page onto the new design

The home page (/) now has the new look. Every other public page still uses the earlier "pen and paper" design: yellow highlighter marks, hard yellow drop-shadows and heavy black grotesk type. This plan rebuilds each of them on the current design system. It does not recolour them.

Based on a walk-through of every route in src/app on the local build, with screenshots of each page. Design coding starts only after this plan is agreed.

The design we are matching

These are the choices the new home page settled on. Every rebuilt page uses the same tokens and components, not lookalikes.

Look

  • Warm paper ground (#FBF8F2), ink text, one orange accent (#FF7A1A).
  • Instrument Serif for display headings, Geist sans for body, mono uppercase eyebrows.
  • Warm "stage" gradients behind product moments; one dark night band per page at most.
  • Soft lift shadows and hairlines. No hard offset shadows, no highlighter marks.

Components

  • Pill buttons with an arrow disc; the white pill link for secondary actions.
  • Lindy-style product cards: white, hairline, status chip, rows that tick.
  • The bento grid: grainy orange hero, photo tile with glass chips, stone, list and coral tiles.
  • Docky as the sender of private cards, with the Instagram and Gmail app logos.

Motion and honesty

  • Reveal on scroll, a status chip that works then flips, rows that tick, money that counts up.
  • Everything readable with motion off; motion respects reduced-motion.
  • Examples labelled as made up; no invented testimonials, logos or stats.
  • Plain writing: no em dashes and no hype words.

/in: the same page, written for India

The India page uses the same design and the same sections as the home page. Only the content changes, and it should read like it was written by people who know Indian creators, not like a translation with flags on it.

How it is built

  • One landing component, two content sets: LANDING_GLOBAL (today) and a new LANDING_IN.
  • Prices come from PLAN_PRICES in INR, already supported by the pricing code.
  • The phone story, inbox section, roster and bento all read from the content set, so nothing is hard-coded to the US example.
  • The "India" bar on the global page links to /in; /in gets the reverse link.

What changes in the content

  • Amounts in rupees, written as Indian creators write them (₹18K, ₹1.2L–₹2.5L).
  • Fictional Indian brands and creators, with natural names and niches (skincare, food, fitness, finance, fashion).
  • Times in IST; a festive-season brand brief where it fits the story.
  • English with light, real Hinglish only where a DM would actually use it. Never in our own voice.

What to avoid

  • No "yaar", "bhai" or Bollywood references in our copy.
  • No festival clichés, flags or saffron colour accents.
  • No made-up local stats or "Trusted by Indian creators" claims.
  • No promises about payments or tax features we have not built (for example GST invoices or UPI collection) until they are confirmed.

Research before writing

  1. Read real creator DMs and briefsCollect anonymised examples from the pilot and from public creator posts about brand outreach in India: how brands open, what they ask for, how rates and usage are phrased.
  2. Check rate ranges against public reportsUse published Indian influencer-pricing reports and agency rate cards to set believable example ranges per follower band. Label them as examples, as today.
  3. Draft LANDING_IN, then reviewWrite the India content set section by section and review it with a creator before it ships. Page copy goes to a branch for founder review; it is not merged without it.

Shared pieces to build once

Tokens out of .landing

Move the colour, type and shadow tokens from landing.module.css into one shared marketing stylesheet, so document pages use exactly the same values.

Page parts

Eyebrow, section head, white pill link, product card, Docky avatar, app logos and bento tiles become small shared components, reused by every page.

Document layout

One new template for long pages: a header on the warm stage, an optional summary card, sticky contents on wide screens, and comfortable 68-character lines.
